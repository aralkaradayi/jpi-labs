About the project

Understanding the changing world of work

Artificial intelligence is reshaping the relationship between education, recruitment and employment. As AI tools become increasingly embedded in professional environments, students and graduates need more than technical familiarity: they need the ability to understand, evaluate and use these tools responsibly.

This project examines AI literacy and its implications for employability, bringing together research and practical guidance for students entering the labour market.

Research and practical resources
AI Literacy and Its Impact on the Job Market

Introduction

Artificial intelligence is changing the nature of work, influencing how organisations operate and how employers recruit candidates. Understanding these developments is increasingly important for students and graduates.

AI Literacy

AI literacy involves understanding the capabilities and limitations of AI systems, evaluating their outputs and applying them appropriately. It also requires awareness of issues such as bias, privacy, transparency and accountability.

Implications for Employment

AI can automate certain tasks while creating demand for new skills. Graduates therefore benefit from combining digital competence with critical thinking, communication, adaptability and subject-specific knowledge.

Responsible Use

Using AI effectively requires human judgement. Candidates should be able to assess generated information, recognise errors and remain responsible for the work they submit.

Company Profiling

Researching an Organisation

Researching an organisation helps candidates move beyond asking whether a role exists to understanding whether the employer is somewhere they want to work — and why.

What to Investigate

  • The organisation's purpose, history and values.
  • Its products, services and position in the market.
  • Recent developments and strategic priorities.
  • Its organisational culture and working environment.
  • The role's responsibilities and required competencies.

Applying Your Research

Effective company research should inform applications, cover letters and interview answers. The aim is to make specific connections between the organisation's work, the position and the candidate's own experience.

Cover Letter

Writing an Effective Cover Letter

A cover letter should explain why you are interested in a particular position and what you can contribute. It should complement your CV rather than simply repeat it.

Key Principles

  • Address the requirements of the specific role.
  • Demonstrate knowledge of the organisation.
  • Use concrete examples to support your claims.
  • Explain your motivation clearly and convincingly.
  • Keep the language professional and concise.
CV

Building a Strong CV

A CV presents a candidate's education, experience, skills and achievements. Its purpose is to communicate relevant information clearly and efficiently.

Key Principles

  • Use a clear and consistent structure.
  • Prioritise experience relevant to the position.
  • Describe responsibilities and achievements precisely.
  • Use active language and specific examples.
  • Check formatting, spelling and accuracy.
Interview

Preparing for an Interview

Interviews allow employers to assess a candidate's experience, motivation, judgement and suitability for a position. Preparation should combine organisational research with reflection on one's own experience.

Preparation Strategies

  • Research the organisation and the position.
  • Prepare examples demonstrating relevant skills.
  • Practise structuring answers using the STAR method.
  • Prepare thoughtful questions for the interviewer.
  • Reflect on what you learned from previous experiences.
